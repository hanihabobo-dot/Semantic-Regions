1. Is that sentence true? issue #157 — fixed

“Implementations often resort to particle filters over dense voxel grids to approximate these continuous belief states [TAMPURA, Bayes3D]…” — is this part true?

No — it was inaccurate, and it’s now fixed.

The sentence pinned one mechanism — “particle filters over dense voxel grids” — on two citations that don’t actually work that way:

  • TAMPURA does not use a particle filter over voxels for object pose. Its voxel grid is a visibility belief; placement is sampled in continuous pose space; and it plans with a learned abstract MDP solved by LAO* — no particle filter.
  • Bayes3D does use sequential Monte Carlo (a particle-filter family), but it is a 3D-scene perception system, not a POMDP-TAMP planner — so it doesn’t belong under “POMDP-based TAMP solutions,” and “dense voxel grid” isn’t its scene model.

The kernel of truth (continuous pose belief is hard; dense/sampling-heavy representations scale poorly) is fine — only the specific mechanism + attribution was wrong. The sentence was rewritten to describe each system accurately.

2. How does TAMPURA deal with space?

How does TAMPURA represent and reason about space?

TL;DR: Three different ways, depending on the job — and none of them is a global place-grid.
  1. Planning state — no spatial grid at all. The planner reasons over an abstract, symbolic MDP (logical predicates), not over geometry. Space is abstracted away before planning starts.
  2. Continuous poses for grounding. Where real geometry matters (placements, grasps), TAMPURA samples continuous SE(3) poses — no discretization, no place lattice.
  3. A voxel grid only for visibility, only in one task. The find_dice environment uses a 15 mm occupancy grid as a belief over what has been seen: voxels start “occupied” (could-be-hidden-here) and flip to “free” as rays confirm sight.
Easy to misread: TAMPURA’s voxels are a visibility/occupancy belief, not a place-grounding lattice and not a pose representation. (That confusion is exactly what caused the #157 error.)

3. Abstract symbolic MDP? LAO*? Are we better?

What is an abstract symbolic MDP and what is a LAO*? Is there a way in which we are better than them?

Abstract symbolic MDP

An MDP (Markov Decision Process) is the standard model for planning under uncertainty: states, actions, transition probabilities, and costs; solving it yields a policy (state → best action).

  • Symbolic — states are logical predicates (holding(cup), visible(region)), not raw geometry. Uncertainty is baked into the state (“die location unknown”), so planning to reduce uncertainty falls out naturally.
  • Abstract — a deliberately coarse model. Each controller’s possible symbolic outcomes and their probabilities are learned by simulation offline. This collapses an infinite continuous problem into a small, finite MDP.

LAO*

LAO* (Hansen & Zilberstein, 2001) is a heuristic search algorithm for solving MDPs whose solutions contain loops (e.g. “look again if you didn’t find it”). It expands only the relevant part of the state space and returns an optimal, contingent, information-gathering policy.

Are we better? — honest answer

Where Boxels genuinely winWhere TAMPURA wins (don’t claim otherwise)
  • Spatial belief scales better — an adaptive, object-centric partition vs. a dense uniform grid.
  • Occlusion is first-class, inspectable symbolic state.
  • No offline per-controller model-learning step.
  • Plan-time speed (offline amortization).
  • Real perception — you currently use a ground-truth oracle.
  • Maturity — fewer hardcoded constants.
Framing: not “we’re better,” but “a different point in the design space — a structured, adaptive, inspectable spatial belief, traded against TAMPURA’s faster amortized planning.”

6. Stage 0 detail · action vs. controller · pick/place/sense · PDDL actions?

Explain Stage 0 in much greater detail; the difference between an action and a controller; how their pick/place/sense differ from ours; and do they even have PDDL actions the planner reasons with?

Action vs. controller

A PDDL action is purely symbolic — a name, parameters, preconditions, and add/delete effects. It executes nothing. A controller is an executable, closed-loop skill plus a model of its (often learned, stochastic) effect. TAMPURA fuses the skill and its model into one controller and plans over those. We separate them: hand-written PDDL actions for the planner, plus separate execute_… routines at runtime — and we don’t model stochasticity, we replan.

Do they have PDDL actions? — yes, but at a different layer

The decision layer is an MDP over controllers (not classical PDDL planning). But to build that MDP, TAMPURA repeatedly constructs optimistic deterministic plans with a classical planner — so a PDDL-style domain is reasoned over underneath. (Which planner? See Q12 — it’s SymK, not FastDownward.)

Sharpest differences: place = discrete boxel vs. continuous pose; sense = optimistic-then-replan vs. probabilistic branching. But our sense is still first-class — the planner can deliberately “move the occluder, then look.”

8. How does TAMPURA decide where to place?

For an action like place, how does TAMPURA reason where to place an object?

TL;DR: It mostly doesn’t reason about “where” symbolically — it delegates “where” to a continuous sampler and lets the planner see only the outcome.
  • No place lattice. The symbolic place effect is coarse (“object down / hand empty”) — the planner never picks among discrete regions.
  • “Where” is a continuous sample. placement_sample_fn samples SE(3) poses in the table area and keeps a feasible one.
  • The planner only sees the learned outcome (place succeeds with some probability), estimated during Learn-Model.

Contrast: for you, “where” is a symbolic choice (place ?o ?b targets a discrete FREE_SPACE boxel); only then does a stream solve IK for the exact pose inside it.

10. Are the voxels individual predicates/fluents?

Are those individual voxels each a fluent or predicate in the belief? (We cloned TAMPURA and read the code.)

No. Voxels never enter the symbolic belief at all — proven from their source.
  • The grid is a plain data structure. VoxelGrid stores occupancy in a Python dict (value_from_voxel) — manipulated procedurally, never as logic.
  • The symbolic belief has zero voxels. The abstract() method emits only object-level atoms (known-pose, holding, is-target, moved, at-home). No voxel predicate exists.
  • Voxels become a probability, not state. In look_effects_fn, the count of newly-visible vs. still-hidden voxels becomes the success/fail probability of the single predicate known-pose(die).
This is the crisp POD-vs-POMDP distinction: TAMPURA keeps occlusion sub-symbolic (a voxel grid feeding one predicate’s probability); you lift it into symbolic state (enumerable SHADOW regions with knowledge-literals the planner branches over). That is a genuine, defensible difference — and the backbone of your contribution.

11. What does the look(?o1,?o2,?q) controller look like?

How does the controller for look(?o1, ?o2, ?q) look?

In TAMPURA a controller = a schema that wires together a planning-time model (effects_fn), a real execution (execute_fn), and a stream for the continuous ?q. Parameters: look behind occluder ?o2 for object ?o1 from camera config ?q.

The planning-time model — where voxels become a probability

def look_effects_fn(action, belief, store):
    # move arm to look-config ?q, render the camera image
    new_seen   = belief.get_new_seen_voxels(camera_image)
    success_count = len(new_seen)                  # voxels this look reveals
    fail_count    = num_occupied - success_count   # voxels still hidden
    # two abstract outcomes, weighted by those counts -> outcome probabilities
    ab_counts = {fail_belief: fail_count, success_belief: success_count}
    return AbstractBeliefSet(ab_counts, belief_map)   # what LAO* reasons over

The real-world controller

def look_execute_fn(action, belief, state, store):
    motion_plan = plan_motion(..., look_conf, ...)   # move the camera there
    state.apply_sequence([motion_plan])
    camera_image = state.world.robot.get_image(...)  # take a real image
    return state, obs_from_camera_image(...)          # feeds belief.update()
This is the action-vs-controller split made concrete: a plain PDDL action would only have the symbolic precondition/effect; TAMPURA’s controller adds an executable skill and a predictive (simulation-based) model of its stochastic outcome.

12. Is FastDownward faster than LAO*? #160 — logged

Is FastDownward faster than LAO* to begin with?

TL;DR: They solve different problems, so it’s not a real race — and checking the code flipped the premise.
  • A deterministic planner finds a plan; LAO* solves an MDP (a policy over a stochastic model). For a comparable size, the MDP solve is strictly more work — but absolute speed depends on the instance.
  • They’re usually layered, not competing: a deterministic planner is a subroutine that builds the model the MDP solver works on.
Discovery: TAMPURA does not use FastDownward. Its deterministic planner is SymK (a Fast Downward–derived symbolic top-k planner), and LAO* solves the MDP. So the thesis line “both systems use FastDownward…the difference is not the search engine” is inaccurate — logged as #160 for fixing (in discussion.tex and THESIS_NOTES §21.3).
The real point: in neither system is the symbolic solver the bottleneck — geometry sampling is. So “FastDownward vs LAO*” is the wrong axis; “when is sampling paid” is the right one.

14. Does TAMPURA reason about space & shadow regions? Nested occluders?

Doesn’t it need to reason about space and shadow regions? What about nested objects — can it find an object behind a series of nested occluders?

TL;DR: It reasons about space, but not in the symbolic planner, and it has no “shadow region” concept. Its benchmark doesn’t test nesting.

Space lives in two places outside the planner: the visibility voxel grid, and the effects_fn simulation that estimates outcome probabilities. The symbolic planner consumes those probabilities; it never reasons over geometry. And TAMPURA has no shadow-region concept — that’s your boxels. The closest it gets is look(?o1, ?o2, ?q) = “look behind object ?o2.”

Nested occluders: their find_dice hides the die under one cup (among 1–5 cups; env_generator.py) — single-layer, never nested. In principle it could chain (move a cup → depth image changes → grid updates), but the abstraction is coarse (one occluder per look; one binary known-pose), and deep nesting is untested. Yours handles nesting by design: view_clear requires clearing all blockers, and a sense that reveals a new occluder spawns a new SHADOW and re-partitions — peeling layers. That makes nested occlusion a clean distinguishing experiment (logged as TAMPURA_PLAN C5).

16. What does “TAMPURA assumes its abstraction” mean?

You said TAMPURA “assumes its abstraction” — what does that mean?

Its policy is only as good as the learned model. TAMPURA trusts that the J-simulation effect probabilities and its chosen abstraction predicates faithfully capture reality. If the abstraction is too coarse, or the samples unrepresentative, LAO* optimizes against a wrong model and can be confidently suboptimal. You don’t have that failure mode — you replan against the current belief each step (you instead assume the oracle).

17. What does an abstract belief state look like — is it a matrix?

How does the symbolic belief look? Is it a matrix? Facts plus a likelihood?

TL;DR: No matrix, no per-fact probability — it’s a set of logical facts (atoms).

TAMPURA’s `abstract()` returns e.g. {known-pose(cup1), is-target(die), moved(cup2), at-home}. Probabilities live on the transitions between whole belief-states (P(land in state A) vs P(B)), not on individual facts. Yours is the PDDL state `_build_init` emits (is_shadow, obj_at_boxel_KIF, view_clear, …) plus a small `BeliefState` dict. The only array-like things in either system are the sub-symbolic substrates (TAMPURA’s voxel dict; your raycast grids) — which the planner never sees.

18. Could TAMPURA solve our find / find-and-stack? Adaptation cost?

What does “gridded” mean? What gets updated on a reveal? Walk me through TAMPURA solving our find / find-and-stack. Does it have similar tasks? What’s the adaptation cost?

  • “Gridded” = discretized onto a fixed lattice (pick a cell). “Sampled, not gridded” = continuous (x,y,z) sampled directly.
  • On reveal: seen voxels flip occupied→free in the dict; if the die is hit, its pose is recorded and known-pose(die) enters the belief.
  • Find (= your holding): this is find_dice (goal: hold the die + at-home). Loop: build MDP (simulate look-behind-each-cup / pick / place) → LAO* policy → execute → observe → replan.

Glossary

TAMP
Task and Motion Planning — combining symbolic task planning with continuous motion planning.
POMDP
Partially Observable Markov Decision Process — a probabilistic model of acting under uncertainty; yields a policy.
POD
Partially Observable Deterministic planning — deterministic actions over knowledge-literals; uncertainty handled by what is known, plus replanning. Your paradigm.
MDP
Markov Decision Process — states, actions, transition probabilities, costs.
LAO*
Heuristic search that solves MDPs whose solutions contain loops (Hansen & Zilberstein 2001). TAMPURA’s MDP solver.
FastDownward
A classical (deterministic) planner using heuristic search. Used by your PDDLStream pipeline.
SymK
A Fast Downward–derived symbolic top-k planner. TAMPURA’s deterministic planner (not FastDownward).
PDDLStream
The TAMP framework you build on — symbolic planning with “streams” that sample continuous parameters.
Boxel
Your semantic spatial unit; the partition has OBJECT, FREE_SPACE, and SHADOW boxels.
Shadow boxel
An explicit symbolic region representing volume occluded behind an object — where a hidden target might be.
Voxel (TAMPURA)
A cell in TAMPURA’s 15 mm visibility grid — a data-structure entry, not a planning predicate.
