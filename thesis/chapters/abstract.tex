\begin{abstract}
This thesis extends classical Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) to partial
observability by integrating a \emph{Semantic Boxel} representation into a
classical TAMP planner. A \emph{Boxel} is a box-shaped cell of the workspace;
the partition concentrates resolution on the objects and the regions they
occlude, covering the free space with a few coarse cells. The robot's belief about which Boxels are occupied is encoded as Know-If
Fluents (one per Boxel, marking whether its contents are known), so a
classical planner can reason about hidden objects and plan sensing actions in
the same search, without enumerating an exhaustive state space. On three
tabletop tasks evaluated against a uniform-grid baseline at the same cell
scale, the adaptive partition produces an order of magnitude fewer cells, cuts
per-call planning time accordingly, and solves find-and-tray-stack scenes far more often than the uniform baseline (75.6\,\% vs 34.4\,\% success).
\end{abstract}
